Interactive Digital Learning Platform for Sri Lankan O/L Students

Abstract

One research team, four connected questions

Most digital study tools used by Sri Lankan O/L students are not aligned to the national curriculum, offer limited Sinhala support, and treat explanation, practice, practical science and revision as separate activities.

This research investigates Nous, an integrated, bilingual learning platform for Grades 10–11 built on four components: a curriculum-grounded learning assistant, adaptive quizzes with mastery tracking, an AI-guided virtual science lab, and handwritten-note analysis.

The aim is an explainable learning loop grounded in the local curriculum, with each component evaluated.

Research components

Four components, one learning loop

Each component is an independent research contribution, integrated through a shared curriculum base and learner profile.

01Learning assistant

AI-driven grade-specific learning assistant

Curriculum retrieval with RAG, English–Sinhala interaction, source attribution, and safe handling when the curriculum has no answer.

  • RAG
  • Bilingual
  • Source attribution
Methodology
02Adaptive assessment

Intelligent quiz-based assessment

Adaptive difficulty and continuous, evidence-weighted mastery using Bayesian Knowledge Tracing, with targeted recommendations.

  • BKT
  • Adaptive
  • Mastery
Methodology
03Virtual laboratory

AI-guided virtual science laboratory

Curriculum-aligned experiments with deterministic outcomes, progressive hints and behaviour-based misconception diagnosis.

  • Simulation
  • Hints
  • Diagnosis
Methodology
04Note understanding

Curriculum-grounded handwritten-note understanding

Bilingual OCR, lesson matching and concept classification (present / partial / missing) with evidence-linked revision support.

  • OCR

01

Literature survey

AI can support learning in four ways relevant to this study.

Conversational AI & RAG

Grounding answers in verified content improves reliability, but Sinhala remains low-resource.

Adaptive assessment

Adjusting difficulty to the learner improves engagement and retention.

Virtual laboratories

Simulated practicals can match physical labs, but rarely diagnose misconceptions.

Note analysis

Vision and generative AI can reveal knowledge gaps from handwritten notes.

02

Research gap

These capabilities exist, but mostly apart and outside the O/L context.

No integration

Tutoring, assessment, labs and note analysis are built and evaluated in isolation.

Limited Sinhala

Few systems are genuinely bilingual for English and Sinhala learners.

Weak curriculum fit

Most tools target higher education, not the Sri Lankan O/L syllabus.

03

Research problem

The question that drives the study.

How can AI provide personalised, interactive and explainable support for Sri Lankan O/L students, while staying aligned with the national curriculum in English and Sinhala?
04

Research objectives

One overall goal delivered through four components.

Main objective

Build and evaluate an integrated, curriculum-grounded AI learning platform for Grade 10–11 students.

  1. AssistantBilingual, curriculum-aligned answers with sources
  2. QuizAdapt practice to each learner's mastery
  3. LabGuide practicals and detect misconceptions
  4. NotesFind concept gaps in handwritten notes

01

Learning assistant

Retrieval-augmented generation over the curriculum, with cited sources and English–Sinhala translation.

02

Quiz assessment

Evidence-weighted mastery and Bayesian Knowledge Tracing drive adaptive difficulty.

03

Virtual lab

Deterministic simulations; Bayesian plan recognition infers misconceptions from actions.

04

Note understanding

OCR and lesson matching, then XLM-RoBERTa labels concepts present, partial or missing.
